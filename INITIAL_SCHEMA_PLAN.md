# Initial Schema Plan

This document defines the first real implementable canonical Supabase/PostgreSQL schema plan for the platform. It is intentionally pragmatic: the goal is a clear relational foundation that a small team can implement, test, and evolve safely with AI-assisted development.

The schema supports canonical identities, contact points, apartment/unit relationships, owner portal authentication, canonical claims/tickets, Chatwoot synchronization, cross-channel communications, and future AI/operator assist and voice/VAPI capabilities without introducing premature platform complexity.

## 1. Canonical Persistence Principles

- Supabase/PostgreSQL is the canonical operational database for the platform.
- Chatwoot conversations are operational artifacts, not canonical business tickets.
- Claims are the canonical business tickets/cases.
- Chatwoot IDs are external references only and must never become platform identifiers.
- Identity resolution happens in the BFF/Supabase layer, not inside Chatwoot and not inside the portal client.
- Simplicity and maintainability are preferred over theoretical purity.
- The schema must support future SaaS evolution without prematurely implementing multi-tenancy complexity.
- Supabase stores the normalized operational projection of Octavo Piso source records, portal authentication state, claim state, message projections, and external channel links.
- External systems such as Octavo Piso, Chatwoot, WhatsApp providers, email systems, and future voice providers may contribute data, but they do not own canonical platform relationships.
- Canonical identifiers are generated and owned by Supabase. External identifiers are stored only as source references for synchronization, reconciliation, and debugging.
- Public owner-facing ticket numbers are generated by the platform and remain stable even if Chatwoot conversations are merged, replaced, archived, or recreated.
- The initial schema should be boring, explicit, and relational. It should be easy to inspect with SQL, easy to explain to operators, and easy for future agents to modify safely.

## 2. Canonical Core Tables

This section describes conceptual tables only. It does not define SQL, constraints, indexes, policies, migrations, triggers, or ORM models.

### persons

`persons` represents real humans who may participate in the platform.

Supported roles include:

- owners
- tenants
- residents
- family members
- future operators/admins

Purpose:

- Store the canonical human identity independent of communication channel.
- Provide the stable anchor for contact points, unit relationships, portal accounts, claim authorship, and future operator/admin identities.
- Avoid duplicating a person just because they appear through email, WhatsApp, phone, Octavo Piso, or Chatwoot.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase as canonical operational identity data.
- Initially populated primarily from Octavo Piso synchronization and later enriched through deterministic BFF flows.
- Chatwoot contact records may reference or mirror persons but do not own them.

Relationships:

- One person may have multiple `contact_points`.
- One person may relate to multiple units through `person_unit_relationships`.
- One unit may relate to multiple persons through `person_unit_relationships`.
- A person may be the `primary_person_id` on many `claims`.
- A person may author visible or internal `claim_messages` when deterministically known.
- A person may have zero or one initial `portal_accounts` record for owner portal access.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `full_name`
- `preferred_name`
- `status`
- `source_system`
- `external_source_id`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- `status` should remain simple initially, for example active, inactive, or pending_review.
- `source_system` and `external_source_id` are synchronization references, not business identity.
- Do not model every possible role directly on `persons`; unit-specific roles belong in `person_unit_relationships`.

### contact_points

`contact_points` represents communication identities associated with canonical persons.

Supported types:

- email
- whatsapp
- phone
- future voice identifiers

Purpose:

- Store normalized communication addresses used for login, identity resolution, outbound delivery, inbound matching, and Chatwoot contact synchronization.
- Make contact channels explicit and queryable without relying on Chatwoot contact data.
- Support multiple communication methods for the same person.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase as canonical contact data.
- Seeded from Octavo Piso and later updated through deterministic verification and reconciliation flows.
- Chatwoot may receive contact projections but does not own the canonical record.

Relationships:

- A person may have multiple contact points.
- A contact point belongs to one active person in normal operation.
- A contact point should not belong to multiple active persons without explicit review/merge handling.
- Contact points are central to identity resolution for portal login, inbound Chatwoot webhooks, email, WhatsApp, phone, and future voice flows.
- `portal_accounts.primary_contact_point_id` points to one approved contact point for authentication delivery.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `person_id`
- `type`
- `normalized_value`
- `raw_value`
- `verified`
- `observed_only`
- `primary_contact`
- `active`
- `source_system`
- `external_source_id`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- `normalized_value` is used for deterministic matching and should be unique enough to prevent unsafe duplicate active ownership.
- `raw_value` preserves the source representation for operator review and debugging.
- `verified` means the platform trusts the contact point for the relevant use case.
- `observed_only` marks records that should not authorize portal access.
- Unknown inbound contacts should normally start in `observed_contacts`, not as verified contact points.

### apartments_or_units

`apartments_or_units` represents apartments, lots, units, or other functional units used for access control and claim context.

Purpose:

- Store the canonical unit context for portal visibility, claims, ownership, tenancy, and Octavo Piso synchronization.
- Normalize source apartment/lote/UF concepts into one simple table for MVP operations.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase as canonical operational unit data.
- Initially populated from Octavo Piso building/apartment synchronization.

Relationships:

- Units belong to buildings/barrios through `building_id`.
- A unit may have many related persons through `person_unit_relationships`.
- A unit may have many `claims`.
- A unit can be visible to multiple portal users when relationship rules allow it.
- External Octavo Piso references must be stored to support idempotent synchronization and reconciliation.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `building_id`
- `unit_code`
- `display_name`
- `active`
- `source_system`
- `external_source_id`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- `unit_code` should preserve the operational unit label, such as apartment UF or lot code.
- `display_name` should be portal/operator friendly.
- Inactive units may remain stored for history but should not automatically grant portal access.

### buildings

`buildings` represents barrios, buildings, consorcios, or comparable grouping entities from Octavo Piso and future sources.

Purpose:

- Store the parent context for units and claims.
- Provide a simple future boundary for reporting, filtering, operational assignment, and eventual SaaS evolution.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase as canonical operational building/consorcio data.
- Initially populated from Octavo Piso or configuration-backed synchronization.

Relationships:

- A building has many `apartments_or_units`.
- A building has many `claims`, usually through unit association but also directly when a claim is building-wide.
- A building may later become part of a more explicit organization/tenant model, but that should not be implemented in the initial schema.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `name`
- `external_source_id`
- `active`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- Keep this table intentionally small.
- Do not introduce a full tenant/account hierarchy yet.

### person_unit_relationships

`person_unit_relationships` represents explicit relationships between persons and units.

Supported relationship examples:

- owner
- tenant
- resident
- spouse/family member
- authorized contact

Purpose:

- Provide the canonical authorization basis for owner portal visibility.
- Represent real-world relationships without overloading `persons` or `contact_points`.
- Support many-to-many relationships between people and units.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase as canonical access-relevant relationship data.
- Initially populated from Octavo Piso and updated through reviewed operational flows.

Relationships:

- Each record links one `person` to one `apartments_or_units` record.
- A person can have multiple active relationships across one or more units.
- A unit can have multiple active relationships for owners, tenants, residents, spouses/family members, and authorized contacts.
- Relationship-based visibility is critical for the portal.
- Multiple active relationships are allowed when they reflect real authorization.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `person_id`
- `unit_id`
- `relationship_type`
- `active`
- `start_date`
- `end_date`
- `source_system`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- Relationship semantics should be explicit and stable.
- Portal rules should use this table rather than channel-specific metadata.
- Future delegated access can extend this model with a separate table only when needed.

### portal_accounts

`portal_accounts` represents authenticated owner portal access for a canonical person.

Purpose:

- Separate portal authentication from Chatwoot authentication.
- Provide a lightweight account record that connects passwordless login to canonical identity.
- Avoid treating Chatwoot users, Chatwoot contacts, or external channel identities as portal accounts.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase/BFF authentication flows.
- Created only for persons who are allowed to access the portal through verified contact points and relationship-based authorization.

Relationships:

- Each portal account belongs to one `person`.
- Each portal account references a `primary_contact_point_id` used for OTP/magic-link delivery.
- A portal account may have many `portal_sessions`.

Authentication approach:

- Portal auth is separate from Chatwoot auth.
- Initial portal auth should use passwordless OTP/magic-link style flows.
- No passwords initially.
- Observed contacts never become portal accounts automatically.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `person_id`
- `primary_contact_point_id`
- `active`
- `last_login_at`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- The account does not decide what the user can see by itself; visibility comes from person-unit relationships.
- Deactivating a portal account should block login without deleting the canonical person.

### portal_sessions

`portal_sessions` represents authenticated sessions/tokens for the owner portal.

Purpose:

- Provide a lightweight MVP session model tied to portal accounts.
- Allow later revocation, expiration, device review, and security hardening without designing a full auth platform now.

Ownership:

- Owned by the BFF/Supabase portal authentication layer.

Relationships:

- Each session belongs to one `portal_account`.
- A portal account may have multiple sessions over time.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `portal_account_id`
- `issued_at`
- `expires_at`
- `revoked`
- `ip_address` later
- `user_agent` later

Notes:

- Keep this simple for MVP.
- Revocation support can be implemented later without changing the identity model.
- IP address and user agent should be added only when useful for security or support operations.

### claims

`claims` is the critical canonical business-ticket table.

Claims are the canonical business tickets/cases.

Explicit rules:

- Claims are NOT Chatwoot conversations.
- Chatwoot conversations are operational workflow references only.
- A claim may aggregate multiple conversations, messages, and channels.
- A claim should survive replacement or removal of Chatwoot as an operational tool.

Purpose:

- Store the canonical owner/business case shown in the portal.
- Provide the stable ticket/case record for operations, synchronization, analytics, AI summaries, and historical continuity.
- Decouple owner-visible status from Chatwoot operational status.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase as canonical business state.
- Created by portal submissions, BFF-normalized inbound channel flows, or reviewed operator workflows.

Relationships:

- A claim belongs to a `building` and usually a specific `unit`.
- A claim has one `primary_person_id` when the main requester or affected person is known.
- A claim may have many `claim_messages`.
- A claim may have many `claim_channel_links`.
- Multiple people may interact regarding the same claim through messages and future participant concepts.

Required capabilities:

- portal visibility
- operational synchronization
- future analytics
- future AI summaries

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `public_ticket_number`
- `title`
- `description`
- `owner_visible_status`
- `operational_status`
- `priority`
- `category`
- `building_id`
- `unit_id`
- `primary_person_id`
- `created_channel`
- `active`
- `opened_at`
- `closed_at`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- `public_ticket_number` should be platform-generated and stable.
- Owner-visible statuses are projections, not raw Chatwoot statuses.
- `operational_status` may be influenced by Chatwoot workflow but should still be stored as a platform-controlled projection.
- Claims should not duplicate Chatwoot assignment, inbox state, labels, or internal workflow details unless a real product requirement emerges.

### claim_channel_links

`claim_channel_links` maps canonical claims to external systems and communication channels.

Examples:

- Chatwoot conversation
- email thread
- WhatsApp thread
- future voice session

Purpose:

- Connect canonical claims to external operational artifacts without making those artifacts canonical.
- Support idempotent webhook processing and synchronization.
- Allow one claim to aggregate multiple external conversations or channel threads.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase/BFF synchronization flows.
- Created when the BFF links a canonical claim to a Chatwoot conversation, email thread, WhatsApp thread, or future voice session.

Relationships:

- Each link belongs to one `claim`.
- A claim may have many channel links.
- External IDs remain scoped by `external_system` and `external_entity_type`.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `claim_id`
- `external_system`
- `external_entity_type`
- `external_entity_id`
- `synchronization_state`
- `last_synced_at`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- This table is intentionally explicit rather than polymorphic application chaos.
- Do not store raw webhook payloads here in the initial schema.
- Synchronization state should remain small and operationally useful, for example synced, pending_retry, or error.

### claim_messages

`claim_messages` represents the canonical normalized communication timeline for a claim.

Explicit rules:

- This is NOT a clone of Chatwoot messages.
- This is the canonical business communication projection.
- It stores only the message data the platform needs for portal timeline, cross-channel continuity, operational context, and future AI summaries.

Purpose:

- Aggregate inbound and outbound communication across portal, email, WhatsApp, Chatwoot operator replies, and future voice/VAPI-derived content.
- Support owner-visible and internal-only timelines using mandatory visibility filtering.
- Provide a stable source for future summaries and analytics without exposing Chatwoot internals.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase/BFF normalization flows.
- Populated by portal submissions, Chatwoot webhooks, outbound message operations, and future channel integrations.

Relationships:

- Each message belongs to one `claim`.
- A message may reference `author_person_id` when the author is deterministically known.
- A message may reference an `external_message_id` for idempotency and synchronization.

Required capabilities:

- inbound/outbound
- owner-visible/internal
- cross-channel aggregation
- future AI summaries
- future attachments

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `claim_id`
- `source_channel`
- `direction`
- `message_type`
- `visibility`
- `author_person_id` nullable
- `external_message_id`
- `content`
- `created_at`

Notes:

- Internal operator notes must never be exposed to portal users.
- `visibility` is mandatory for every message.
- `content` should be plain enough for MVP. Avoid unnecessary JSON blobs.
- Attachments should not be implemented here yet beyond future-compatible references in later work.

### observed_contacts

`observed_contacts` represents unknown or unverified inbound contact points.

Examples:

- unknown email
- unknown WhatsApp number
- unknown phone number from future voice calls

Purpose:

- Preserve evidence from inbound channels without granting identity, authorization, or portal access.
- Support later review, linking, and reconciliation into real persons/contact points.
- Avoid unsafe automatic creation of verified contacts from arbitrary inbound messages.

Ownership:

- Owned by Supabase/BFF inbound normalization flows.
- Created when deterministic identity matching fails or when a contact point is ambiguous.

Relationships:

- An observed contact may later be linked to a real `person` through `linked_person_id`.
- Observed contacts do not automatically create `portal_accounts`.
- Observed contacts may be associated with messages or channel links in later implementation if needed, but no extra table is required for the initial schema plan.

Recommended conceptual fields:

- `id`
- `contact_type`
- `normalized_value`
- `first_seen_channel`
- `first_seen_at`
- `linked_person_id` nullable
- `resolved`
- `created_at`
- `updated_at`

Notes:

- Observed contacts do not automatically gain portal access.
- They may later be linked to real persons.
- Unknown inbound contacts should remain safe, visible for operations, and non-authoritative.

### ai_summaries_future

`ai_summaries_future` is a placeholder concept only. It should not be implemented in the MVP schema.

Purpose:

- Reserve conceptual space for AI-generated summaries without designing a premature AI subsystem.

Future AI-generated summaries may exist at:

- person level
- claim level
- timeline level

Notes:

- No implementation details yet.
- Do not create this table initially.
- Do not introduce vector storage, prompt storage, model metadata, or AI audit tables until concrete product workflows require them.
- Future summaries must obey the same visibility and identity rules as the canonical data they summarize.

## 3. Canonical Relationship Principles

### persons ↔ contact_points

- `persons` is the canonical human identity.
- `contact_points` are channel-specific communication identities for that person.
- A person may have many contact points across email, WhatsApp, phone, and future voice identifiers.
- A contact point should belong to only one active person unless explicitly placed into review/merge handling.
- Identity resolution is relational, not channel-centric.

### persons ↔ units

- Persons connect to units through `person_unit_relationships`.
- Do not store unit access directly on the portal account, contact point, or Chatwoot contact.
- One person may relate to multiple units.
- One unit may relate to multiple persons.
- Relationship type determines the starting point for portal visibility and operational interpretation.

### claims ↔ units

- Claims should reference the affected unit whenever deterministically known.
- Unit association is the primary basis for portal visibility.
- Building-level claims may reference a building with a nullable unit only if the implementation explicitly supports building-wide cases later.
- In the MVP, prefer requiring a unit for owner-created portal claims.

### claims ↔ persons

- `primary_person_id` identifies the main requester, reporter, or affected person when known.
- Other people may interact with the same claim through messages and future participant concepts.
- Multiple people may interact regarding the same claim.
- Do not force every claim message to have a known person; unknown or system-originated messages can have nullable authors.

### claims ↔ channel links

- `claim_channel_links` connects claims to external operational artifacts.
- A claim may have one Chatwoot conversation at first, but the schema allows more than one channel link when needed.
- Chatwoot conversation IDs, email thread IDs, WhatsApp thread IDs, and future voice session IDs remain external references.

### claims ↔ messages

- `claim_messages` is the canonical normalized timeline for a claim.
- Messages are channel-normalized and visibility-filtered.
- The same person may communicate through multiple channels.
- A claim may include portal messages, inbound email, WhatsApp, operator replies, system messages, and future voice summaries or transcripts when approved.

## 4. Portal Visibility Principles

### Owner visibility

- Owners see claims associated with units where they have an active owner relationship.
- Owner visibility is based on `person_unit_relationships`, claim-unit association, and message visibility.
- Owners see owner-visible claim status, not raw Chatwoot status.

### Tenant visibility

- Tenants may see claims for units where they have an active tenant relationship when product rules allow tenant visibility.
- Tenant visibility may be narrower than owner visibility.
- Tenant access should be represented by relationship type and explicit portal rules, not by channel metadata.

### Spouse/family visibility

- Spouse and family member access should be modeled as explicit person-unit relationships.
- Family members should remain distinct persons, not duplicate owners and not shared contact aliases.
- Access can be allowed or restricted based on relationship type and active status.

### Future delegated access

- Delegated access should be added later only when a real workflow requires it.
- The likely future shape is a small explicit delegation model tied to persons and units, not an ACL engine.
- Until then, relationship-based visibility remains the primary model.

Rules:

- Visibility is relationship-based.
- Portal users never query raw Chatwoot entities.
- Owner portal reads canonical Supabase projections only.
- Portal responses must filter messages by visibility.
- Observed contacts and unresolved identities receive no portal visibility until verified and authorized.

## 5. Chatwoot Synchronization Principles

### Chatwoot contact synchronization

- Supabase canonical persons and verified/approved contact points are projected into Chatwoot contacts.
- Chatwoot contact IDs are stored as external references only, likely through contact synchronization metadata or a future external references mechanism if necessary.
- Supabase canonical contact data wins when Chatwoot and Supabase differ.
- Chatwoot-originated unknown contacts must be treated as observations before becoming canonical contact points.

### Chatwoot conversation linkage

- Chatwoot conversations link to canonical claims through `claim_channel_links`.
- A Chatwoot conversation is an operational inbox/workflow artifact.
- A claim is the canonical business ticket/case.
- One claim may aggregate multiple Chatwoot conversations if operational reality requires it.

### Webhook normalization

- Chatwoot webhooks are normalized by the BFF into channel-neutral claim and message operations.
- Webhook handlers should be idempotent using external conversation and message identifiers.
- Raw webhook structure should not leak into portal APIs or canonical portal views.

### Status projection

- Chatwoot operational status may influence `operational_status` and `owner_visible_status`, but raw Chatwoot statuses must not be shown directly.
- Owner-visible statuses are safe projections using business language.
- Status mapping must be deterministic and explainable.

### Message synchronization boundaries

- `claim_messages` stores the canonical business communication projection, not every Chatwoot message field.
- Internal Chatwoot notes map only to internal visibility when retained at all.
- Owner-visible messages require explicit visibility classification.
- Attachments are deferred until a later attachment storage plan.

Rule:

- Chatwoot remains operational inbox/workflow only.
- Platform state must survive Chatwoot replacement later if necessary.

## 6. Message Visibility Model

Message visibility is mandatory and must be applied before any portal response is returned.

### owner-visible

- Safe for external portal users with authorized relationship-based access.
- Includes portal-submitted messages, approved operator replies, safe system messages, and future approved summaries.

### internal-only

- Visible only to operators/admin tooling.
- Includes internal operator notes, private operational context, unresolved identity observations, and synchronization diagnostics when represented as messages.
- Internal operator notes must never be exposed externally.

### system-generated

- Created by the platform for deterministic status/timeline events when useful.
- May be owner-visible or internal-only depending on content.
- Should be concise and avoid leaking implementation details.

### future AI-generated summaries

- AI summaries may become owner-visible only after explicit product and review rules are defined.
- Internal AI assist content should default to internal-only.
- AI output must never bypass the same visibility filters that apply to human-authored content.

Rules:

- Visibility filtering is mandatory.
- Internal notes are never exposed externally.
- Portal APIs must query only owner-visible messages for portal users.
- Future analytics and summaries must respect message visibility.

## 7. Identity Resolution Principles

### Matching by email

- Normalize email addresses before matching.
- Match against active canonical email `contact_points`.
- Prefer verified contact points for portal authentication and high-confidence identity resolution.
- Unknown email addresses create `observed_contacts`, not verified persons.

### Matching by phone

- Normalize phone numbers into a deterministic canonical format before matching.
- Match against active phone `contact_points`.
- Ambiguous matches should remain unresolved or require review.
- A phone number should not authorize portal access unless it is verified and tied to an authorized person/unit relationship.

### Matching by WhatsApp

- Normalize WhatsApp numbers using the same deterministic phone normalization rules.
- Match WhatsApp-specific contact points first when present.
- Fall back to phone contact points only when rules allow and ambiguity is absent.
- Unknown WhatsApp numbers create `observed_contacts`.

### Observed contact handling

- Observed contacts are evidence, not authorization.
- Observed contacts can help operators understand inbound messages.
- Observed contacts may later be linked to real persons through explicit review.
- Observed contacts do not automatically create portal accounts or active contact points.

### Future merge/reconciliation flows

- Merge and reconciliation should be explicit, reviewable operations.
- External references should be preserved when identities are merged.
- Ambiguous duplicate contacts should not be resolved by fuzzy matching in the initial implementation.
- Deterministic/simple rules come first.
- Avoid fuzzy AI matching initially.

## 8. Explicit Simplicity Constraints

The initial schema intentionally excludes the following:

- no multi-tenant isolation yet
- no RLS complexity yet
- no ACL engine yet
- no event sourcing
- no CQRS
- no distributed transactions
- no polymorphic ORM-heavy abstractions
- no advanced audit framework yet
- no attachment storage system yet
- no workflow engine yet

Additional constraints:

- Do not create broad generic `entities` tables.
- Do not hide important business relationships inside JSON blobs.
- Do not mirror Chatwoot's entire schema.
- Do not implement a permissions matrix before the owner/tenant/family relationship model proves insufficient.
- Do not implement advanced AI persistence before the canonical claim/message model is working.
- Do not introduce service boundaries or microservices for initial schema concerns.

## 9. Future Compatibility Notes

### AI/operator assist

- The canonical person, unit, claim, message, and channel-link model provides enough structure for future AI/operator assist.
- AI can summarize claims and timelines from canonical projections instead of scraping Chatwoot directly.
- Internal AI suggestions should remain separate from owner-visible messages until reviewed or explicitly published.

### Analytics

- Claims, statuses, categories, priorities, buildings, units, and timestamps support initial operational analytics.
- The schema avoids event-sourcing complexity but still preserves enough relational history for common reporting.
- Later analytics tables or views can be added without changing the canonical transaction model.

### VAPI/voice

- Future voice calls can enter through `claim_channel_links` as voice sessions.
- Voice transcripts or call summaries can become `claim_messages` only after normalization and visibility classification.
- Caller identity should use the same phone/WhatsApp contact-point matching model.

### SaaS evolution later

- `buildings` provides a natural future boundary for organizations, customers, or portfolios.
- Future multi-tenancy can be introduced deliberately after product-market and operational needs are clearer.
- The initial schema should not pretend to solve SaaS isolation before there are real tenant boundaries and operational policies.

### Attachment systems later

- Attachments are intentionally deferred.
- Later attachment storage can link to claims and claim messages using explicit tables.
- Attachment visibility must follow message and claim visibility rules.

### Vector search later if needed

- Vector search can be added later for claims, messages, summaries, or knowledge retrieval.
- It should not be part of the initial operational schema.
- Any vector data must respect visibility boundaries and source-of-truth rules.

## 10. Recommended MVP Implementation Order

Implement the smallest stable vertical slice first. The first goal is not full feature coverage; it is a deterministic path from Octavo Piso source data into canonical Supabase records, then into portal-visible claims and Chatwoot-linked operations.

Recommended order:

1. `buildings`
2. `apartments_or_units`
3. `persons`
4. `contact_points`
5. `person_unit_relationships`
6. `claims`
7. `claim_messages`
8. `claim_channel_links`
9. `portal_accounts`
10. `portal_sessions`
11. `observed_contacts`

Implementation guidance:

- Start with buildings and units so every identity and claim can resolve to a stable operational context.
- Add persons and contact points next so identity resolution can work without Chatwoot dependency.
- Add person-unit relationships before portal auth so visibility is based on canonical relationships from the start.
- Add claims and messages before deep Chatwoot synchronization so the business ticket model remains canonical.
- Add channel links once claim creation and message projection exist.
- Add portal accounts and sessions only after the person/contact/relationship model can authorize access safely.
- Add observed contacts before opening inbound channels broadly so unknown senders remain safe and reviewable.
